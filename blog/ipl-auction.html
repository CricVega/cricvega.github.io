The IPL Auction Explained: Retentions, Purses and Bidding Wars

📅 2026-10-03 • CricVega Blog • 4 min read • All articles

The IPL auction is where squads are built, fortunes change and franchises play chess with money. Here is how it actually works.

The purse

Before each auction cycle, the league fixes a salary cap — a maximum total spend per team (the "purse"). Every rupee spent on a player comes out of this purse, so a giant bid for one star automatically limits what a team can spend later. Franchises enter the auction with a calculated remaining purse after retentions.

Retentions

Teams may keep a limited number of players from their previous squad before the auction, at predefined or negotiated prices depending on the retention rules for that cycle. Retaining a captain or a proven match-winner costs purse money but provides continuity — the classic trade-off between stability and flexibility.

Player pools and sets

Players register and declare a base price. The auction itself runs in sets — open pools, then specialist categories (batters, all-rounders, bowlers, wicketkeepers), capped players, uncapped players, and finally accelerated rounds where teams can re-nominate players who went unsold earlier.

The bidding war

The auctioneer opens a lot at the base price; franchises raise paddles (or press buttons) to bid. Increments grow with the price. When only one paddle remains, the hammer falls and the player joins that team. Teams often pre-agree strategies — targeting specific roles, building combinations around retained players, or ambushing rivals' plans late in a set.

Uncapped and overseas slots

Squads have limits on overseas players in the squad and in the playing XI, which shapes strategy: an expensive overseas quick may force hard choices elsewhere. Uncapped Indian players frequently become the best value picks, and breakout auction stories are usually uncapped all-rounders who fit multiple roles.

Why auctions matter beyond the IPL

The auction model has spread to other leagues around the world and has changed cricket economics: a strong season of domestic cricket can transform a player's career overnight when franchises compete openly for them. For fans, auction day is its own tournament — spreadsheets, rumours and last-second hammer falls.
